EQ Filter

The movable dots each represent a filter used to equalize (or EQ) the audio. Filters allow you to boost or lower certain frequencies (i.e. just part of the treble or bass).

Purple dots act as shelf filters — they boost or cut everything above (high shelf) or below (low shelf) the selected frequency. Blue-green dots act as peaking/notch filters — they affect a narrow band around the selected frequency.

Move a dot left or right to select lower or higher frequencies. Move a dot up or down to increase or decrease the volume of those frequencies.

Presets

Save your favorite EQ configurations as presets to quickly switch between them.

Type a name in the preset text box and click + Save Preset or press ENTER.

Click a saved preset to load it. Use - Delete Preset to remove the selected preset.

Export Presets saves all your presets to a JSON file. Import Presets loads presets from a file.

FAQ

How do I EQ a tab?
Click the goatEQ icon in your toolbar, then click EQ Current Tab. The tab's audio will be processed through the equalizer.

How do I stop EQing?
Click the goatEQ icon again and click Stop EQing This Tab. Or go to the Active Tabs tab and click Stop EQing for any tab.

Why is there no sound?
Make sure the tab is actively playing audio. Some sites need playback to start before audio is available.

Does this work with any site?
Any browser tab with audio content should work — YouTube, Spotify, SoundCloud, Twitch, etc.

Keyboard Shortcuts

Shift + Drag a filter dot up or down to adjust the Q value (width) of the filter. A higher Q means a narrower frequency range.

Double-click a filter dot to reset that filter to 0 dB (flat).

Click Reset Filters to reset all filters at once.

⚠️ Health & Safety Warnings

Hearing Damage Risk: Loud audio can cause permanent hearing damage. Always start at a low volume and gradually increase. Prolonged exposure to high volumes may lead to tinnitus or hearing loss.

Speaker/Headphone Damage: Excessive bass boost or high gain can damage speakers, headphones, or subwoofers. Be especially careful with high-powered audio equipment.

Sudden Volume Spikes: Switching presets or adjusting filters can cause sudden volume changes. Lower your volume before making adjustments.

Clipping & Distortion: Pushing gain too high causes clipping, which can damage both hearing and equipment. Monitor the visualizer for distortion.

Listen responsibly. If you experience ringing in your ears, reduce volume immediately.
